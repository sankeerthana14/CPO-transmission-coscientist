Before the genome confirms, where should we look?

AI-assisted multimodal source prioritisation for hospital-acquired carbapenemase-producing organism (CPO) transmission.

An evidence-grounded approach that ranks plausible patient and environmental sources, maps shared genomic patterns, and supports local, natural-language investigation — without equating a ranking with proof of transmission.

01 / The question

Turning surveillance into investigable hypotheses.

When a patient acquires a carbapenemase gene during hospitalisation, investigators must weigh possible earlier patient and environmental sources — often before genomic confirmation is available.

?

THE SUBMITTED CONFERENCE ABSTRACTRead the full abstract⌄

Hospital transmission investigations for carbapenemase-producing organisms (CPOs) combine epidemiology, microbiology, environmental surveillance and whole-genome sequencing (WGS), but WGS is retrospective and often unavailable when infection-prevention decisions are made, motivating patient-level prediction approaches. We investigated whether an AI-assisted framework could prioritise plausible patient and environmental sources and identify shared dissemination patterns, with WGS retained as an independent reference rather than a predictor. Patient-derived training data also raise confidentiality concerns.

The framework combines machine-learning source prioritisation, WGS assessment of relatedness and network analysis, with an agentic interface for natural-language investigation. A verified knowledge base (LLM wiki) over the WGS and linkage tables provides a cross-linked layer for that interface. Differential privacy (DP) feasibility was explored.

Eight initially CPO-negative patients acquiring a carbapenemase gene during hospitalisation were analysed; temporally eligible prior CPO-positive patients and environmental samples generated 1,274 candidate source–target pairs. Nine features covered timing, source type, GeneXpert and isolate-level carbapenemase-family concordance, exact-gene concordance, species and sequence type. Pairs were mapped to supplied WGS clonal- and plasmid-linkage data; 999 prior, deduplicated, WGS-evaluable pairs (14 genomically linked) were retained. XGBoost used leave-one-target-out validation, with WGS linkage as reference label only, excluded from inputs, and SHAP provided interpretation. A temporally constrained patient–environment network identified recurrent sources and shared clusters. LangGraph orchestrates locally hosted MedGemma and authenticated Model Context Protocol (MCP) tools for natural-language case requests; explanations are checked against outputs without changing scores. The tables became 1,117 cross-linked pages, each with a fixed facts record. Locally generated summaries were published only if every claim matched that record. Random-split gradient boosting with Gaussian-noised leaf statistics compared pair- and patient-level calibration (ε=0.5–16; 30 seeds), using observed maximum patient contributions without enforcing a bound.

The model achieved AUROC 0.963, AUPRC 0.311, mean reciprocal rank 0.688 and Hit@5 1.00 for the four WGS-linked targets; exact-gene concordance was the strongest feature. Four of eight acquisitions had direct WGS-supported source evidence (two patient plasmid, one patient clonal, one environmental clonal); three were unconfirmed and one lacked target WGS. Network analysis identified four recurrent hub sources and a patient cluster spanning three cases. In one case, two environmental samples ranked first and second on timing, species, sequence type and exact-gene concordance yet lacked WGS linkage. Synthetic checks confirmed execution with scores preserved; usability was not assessed. Without DP noise AUROC was 0.92; pair-level calibration gave 0.90 (ε=8) versus ≤0.48 patient-level. Wiki verification rejected all corrupted summaries and admitted 25/42.

AI-assisted prioritisation and network analysis can organise surveillance data into clinically interpretable transmission hypotheses while preserving uncertainty, acting as a co-pilot rather than replacing genomic investigation. Blinded external validation will assess pre-WGS investigation using patient-movement and microbiological data. Future work will add local RAG citations, evaluate explanation accuracy and usability, extend to larger local models and enforce patient-level contribution bounding.
